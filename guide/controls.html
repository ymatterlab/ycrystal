Controls and display¶

Use the mouse and view shortcuts over the 3D viewport. VTK's default keys such as q (quit) and w (wireframe) are disabled. Help ▸ Mouse Controls (F1) opens the in-app summary.

Input Action
Left drag Orbit about the view centre (or turn about the chosen axis, see below)
Middle drag or Shift + left drag Pan
Wheel or right drag Zoom
F / toolbar Fit Fit the displayed geometry, preserving orientation
View ▸ Reset View Restore the default orientation and fit
Left click without dragging Select the front-most ball; empty space clears
Ctrl + click Add/remove a ball from the selection
Esc Clear selection (in a measurement mode: cancel the incomplete measurement)
Distance / Bond angle (View along toolbar, Edit menu) Measure between clicked atoms; see Measurements
Edit ▸ Vectors… Attach direction or magnetic-moment arrows to atoms; see Vectors
Delete or Backspace Hide selected displayed atoms
Ctrl+M Material for the selected atoms (Edit ▸ Material for Selection…)
Drag an a, b, or c handle Add or remove whole cells along that axis
Esc or right button during a handle drag Cancel the drag
Drag a light handle in Lighting Setup Set the key-light direction (one undo step; Esc cancels)
Ctrl+Left / Ctrl+Right Turn by the step angle about the rotation axis
Ctrl+Z Undo a display-setting change
Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y Redo a display-setting change
Ctrl+O Open a structure or project
Ctrl+S / Ctrl+Shift+S Save Project / Save Project As…
F5 POV-Ray preview of the framed view
Ctrl+R Render Image… as PNG, PDF, or JPEG (toolbar Cancel Render stops it)
Ctrl+Q Quit (asks about unsaved changes)

On macOS, read Ctrl as ⌘ (Command) throughout: ⌘ + click toggles the selection (Control + click is a right click there), ⌘Z undoes, ⌘R renders. The function keys F1 and F5 may need the Fn key, depending on the keyboard settings. A mouse or trackpad without a middle button pans with Shift + left drag.

Boundary atoms and hiding¶

View ▸ Show Boundary Atoms is on by default. Atoms are wrapped along periodic cell directions and repeated on equivalent faces, edges, and corners. A corner atom can appear eight times. Turning the option off shows each input atom once per repeated cell at its original position plus the cell translation. Repetition controls expand the display from the original structure without changing the scientific atom count.

Each ball has its own identity: the source atom (0-based internally, 1-based in the status bar) and an integer image offset (na, nb, nc). The offset is relative to the input position. A selected ball's Cartesian position is input_position + image_offset @ cell, with lattice vectors as cell rows.

Delete hides only selected balls, including from POV exports and renders. Other images of the same atom stay visible. View ▸ Show All Atoms restores all hidden IDs. Hidden IDs survive toggling boundary images off and on. Selection highlights are viewport overlays and do not appear in the figure.

Undo/redo covers every change to the figure: hiding, Show All Atoms, boundary images, repetitions (including handle drags), cell-edge modes, bond settings, atom size and element radii, materials (default, element, and atom), element colours, environments, backgrounds, floors, lighting, the output size, and style imports. A handle drag or a style import is one step; consecutive spin-box adjustments of the same value merge into one step. Camera navigation (orbit, pan, zoom, rotation, view directions) is not on the undo stack; View ▸ Reset View returns to the default orientation. File opens and saves are not undoable; opening a new file clears the undo history. A changed camera still marks the project modified. The aspect-ratio lock in the Render tab is saved in projects but is not an undo step; see lock aspect ratio.

Output framing¶

The output size is set in Scene ▸ Render (width and height, 16 to 8000 pixels, with aspect presets). A blue frame in the 3D view marks the rendered area; outside it the view is dimmed. Resizing the window moves the frame on screen but does not change the image. Projection is orthographic; atoms do not shrink with distance from the camera. See output size and frame.

The structure's coordinates, occupancies, and movable flags are scientific data. Camera, radii, colours, boundary images, hiding, materials, background, and lighting are presentation state; see Materials, backgrounds, and lighting for the Scene ▸ Appearance tab and the galleries. There is no atom editor.

Supercells, bonds, and directions¶

The Scene dock has Structure, Bonds, Appearance, Legend (the element legend), and Render tabs. If closed, reopen it from View ▸ Scene. Under Structure, set repetitions along a, b, c (1 to 20), use + all / − all, or reset with 1 × 1 × 1. Typed counts apply on Enter or focus change. The view refits after a repetition change. Expansions above 20,000 displayable atoms ask for confirmation before building geometry. Non-periodic axes are not repeated. Hidden IDs persist across expansion and shrinking. Atom size sets the sphere radius as a multiple of the covalent radius (per-element radii are in the Appearance tab).

Supercell handles¶

Coloured a (red), b (green), and c (blue) handles sit outside the repeated block, on the line through the centre of its far face along each lattice direction. Drag a handle outward to add cells along that axis, inward to remove them (at least one cell remains). While you drag, an orange outline shows the new block and a label gives the cell counts and the number of atoms; orbiting is disabled. The geometry is rebuilt when you release the button, as one undoable step; Esc or the right button cancels the drag. Handles are hidden for non-periodic axes, for structures without a cell, and for directions that point almost at the viewer (rotate the view to reach them). View ▸ Supercell Handles switches them off. Handles exist only in the live view; they never appear in exports or renders.

Cell edges offers All unit cells, Outline of the repeated block, Original cell only, or None. The boundary option applies to the repeated block.

Under Bonds, Show bonds controls visibility. Max. length ranges from 0.80 through 1.50 times the sum of covalent radii; Radius ranges from 0.02 through 0.50 Å. Element-pair checkboxes override the default rule: metal–metal pairs start off in compounds containing non-metals. The pair list reports candidates and length ranges per original cell, not the number drawn in the repeated block. A bond appears only when both endpoint atoms are displayed; hiding an atom removes its bonds. These are geometric contacts, not calculated chemical bond orders.

The View along toolbar offers a, b, c and x, y, z. The selected positive axis points toward the viewer; hold Shift when clicking to reverse it. Screen up is the first of c, b, a (for x, y, z: z, y, x) that is not parallel to the chosen axis, so looking down a or b puts c up and looking down c puts b up. a, b, c are the real-space lattice vectors, not normals of lattice planes: in a skewed cell, looking down c is not the same as looking at the (001) plane face-on, and lattice directions differ from Cartesian directions. These actions fit the view and affect the camera only; they are outside the display-setting undo stack. ycrystal --view uses exactly the same rule (see choose the view).

Rotation modes¶

The Rotate menu in the View along toolbar chooses how a left drag rotates:

  • freely (default): trackball rotation in all directions.
  • about a, b, c (lattice vectors) or about x, y, z (Cartesian axes): the rotation is restricted to that axis through the view centre. The axis keeps its direction on screen while the structure turns around it. Drag across the axis as it appears on screen; when the axis points at you, move the mouse in a circle around the view centre instead.

The ⟲ and ⟳ buttons (Ctrl+Left, Ctrl+Right) turn by the angle chosen next to them (5° to 90°) about the rotation axis, or about the line of sight in free mode, for exact orientations. Clicks still select atoms, and Shift + left drag still pans. Rotation changes only the view: coordinates are unchanged, and the mode is not saved in projects. Lattice axes are unavailable for structures without a cell.

Measurements¶

Distance and Bond angle in the View along toolbar (also Edit ▸ Measure Distance and Edit ▸ Measure Bond Angle) switch from selecting to measuring. The two modes exclude each other; click the active button again to return to ordinary selection. Navigation is unchanged: a drag still orbits, pans, or zooms and never picks an atom, and only a click without dragging counts.

  • Distance: click atom A, then atom B. The result is their separation in Å.
  • Bond angle: click A, then B, then C. The result is the angle A–B–C in degrees (0° to 180°) with B as the vertex, from the arms A − B and C − B. No bond needs to be drawn between the atoms.

The bold text in the status bar says which atom to click next, for example “Bond angle: select atom B — angle vertex”. The picked atoms get a teal wire cage and a boxed letter (A, B, or “B vertex”); a dashed line joins them, and an angle shows a small arc at the vertex. Esc cancels an incomplete measurement and removes these marks (a second Esc clears the ordinary selection); switching modes also cancels it. After a measurement the mode stays on, so the next click starts a new one. Clicking empty space keeps the atoms picked so far; clicking the same ball twice in one measurement is refused with an explanation.

Which atoms are measured. Each ball is one displayed instance: a source atom and its periodic image offset [na nb nc] (see Boundary atoms and hiding). Ycrystal measures exactly the instances you click, at input_position + [na nb nc] @ cell, also in supercells, with boundary images, and in non-orthogonal cells. It does not replace them by the nearest periodic image (no minimum-image convention): to measure across a cell boundary, click the image on the other side. Two images of the same atom are different balls and can be measured against each other. The values come from the structure's coordinates and cell only, so the camera, zoom, atom size, materials, and lighting never change them.

The Measurements panel opens at the bottom of the window, under the 3D view, with the first completed measurement (View ▸ Measurements shows or hides it later). Each row gives the type, the atoms (element, input label, 1-based atom number, and image, e.g. A: O #3 [0 0 0] · B (vertex): Ti #2 [0 0 0] · C: O #4 [0 1 0]), and the result, e.g. 1.9525 Å or 90.00° — vertex B. New measurements are added below the earlier ones. Distances show four decimals and angles two; the full value is in the tooltip, and the decimals do not express an uncertainty. Copy Selected (or Ctrl+C in the table) copies the selected rows as text with atoms, units, and the vertex, for example Angle A–B–C: 90.00° — vertex B (A = O #3 [0 0 0], B = Ti #2 [0 0 0], C = O #4 [0 0 0]). Remove Selected and Clear All delete rows.

Measurements are inspection data for the current session: they are not saved in projects, styles, or exports, and the marks never appear in the POV-Ray preview, the rendered PNG, or the .pov file; they do not affect Fit, the lights, or picking. Opening another structure or project clears all measurements. When a measured ball disappears from the display (boundary atoms off, fewer repetitions, hiding), the measurements that use it are removed and the panel says so; the others stay, because each identity still denotes the same position in the same structure. Undoing the change does not bring removed measurements back.

Vectors (arrows on atoms)¶

Edit ▸ Vectors… (or Vectors… in the Appearance tab) attaches arrows to atoms, for example to illustrate a magnetic structure. You enter the vectors yourself; Ycrystal does not compute, read, or infer magnetic moments.

What a vector is. Three Cartesian components in the structure's own right-handed frame, the frame of the atom positions (not fractional or lattice coordinates, and not a DFT code's spin axes). The camera never changes them. Each vector has a meaning:

  • Magnetic moment: components in Bohr magnetons (μB);
  • Direction: a dimensionless illustration, never presented as a measured moment.

The table shows the components and the modulus. A zero vector is kept as data (modulus 0) and draws no arrow; vectors with a modulus up to 10⁻⁹ count as zero.

Attaching. Select atoms in the 3D view, then Add/Set for Selection…. The editor asks where the vector goes:

  • Selected displayed atom(s) only: exactly those balls (source atom and periodic image), as an image override;
  • All periodic images of their source atom(s): a source default that every image of the atom shows, with the same components in every cell.

An image override takes precedence over its source default, and Suppress on Selected Images removes the arrow from chosen balls even if their source has a default. Each ball shows at most one vector. Atoms of the same element are never grouped: two iron atoms can carry opposite moments. Ycrystal infers no alternating signs, propagation vector, or magnetic symmetry, so an antiferromagnetic pattern has to be assigned explicitly (per source atom, per image, or with a magnetic repeat cell as the structure).

While the editor is open, the 3D view and the POV-Ray preview show the new vector without an undo step; selecting other atoms does not change the editor's targets. Apply is one undo step (also for many atoms at once), Cancel restores the previous state.

The table lists every source default, image override, and suppression with its atom, image, components, meaning, modulus, and how many displayed balls show it. Choosing rows selects their balls in the 3D view. Edit… changes the components of the chosen rows, Reverse negates them, Show/Hide hides arrows without deleting the data, and Remove deletes rows (removing an image override brings back the source default). Each action is one undo step.

Arrow appearance (all arrows): Placement Centred on atom (the default; the arrow runs from r − L u/2 to r + L u/2) or Tail at atom centre (r to r + L u), where u is the unit direction and L the total length, head included. Length is either uniform (L in Å for every arrow) or proportional (L = scale × modulus, with scale in Å per μB for moments and Å per unit for directions). Switching never changes the components. Shaft radius, head length and radius, colour, and material (built-in or one of your custom materials) are separate from the atoms; materials with their own colours ignore the arrow colour. On short arrows the head takes at most 60 % of the length and the whole arrow gets thinner in proportion; the length itself is never changed. A short arrow can be hidden inside its atom: make it longer or the atom smaller.

In figures. Arrows are part of the figure: the POV-Ray preview, the PNG, and the .pov file contain them (as a cylinder and a cone each, with a comment naming the atom and the components), lit and shadowed like the atoms. Fit and the render keep whole arrows in view, including their heads; adding or editing a vector does not move the camera by itself. The lights stay where they are (they depend on the atoms and cell only). Arrows are never picked by clicks and do not change measurements, bonds, or atom counts.

What happens when the display changes. Hiding an atom hides its arrow. Fewer repetitions hide the arrows of images that are no longer displayed, without deleting their overrides; more repetitions bring them back. Changing atom radii does not change arrows. Opening another structure starts without vectors.

Saving. Projects store all vectors at full precision (since project schema 7), including suppressions and the arrow appearance, and the definition of a custom arrow material. Styles carry only the arrow appearance (with the Materials, colours, and radii part); they never transfer vectors to another structure, and importing a style keeps the current structure's vectors. examples/vectors-synthetic-afm.ycrystal shows a small synthetic two-site cell with opposite moments assigned by hand.
